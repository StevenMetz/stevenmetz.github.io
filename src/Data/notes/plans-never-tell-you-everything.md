---
title: The plans never tell you everything
date: 2026-10-06
---
Some blueprints don't come with engineering prints. You still have to know that what you're building can't just sit on anything.

## The porch

On one custom house I was building, there was no structural plan for a porch overhang. The trusses extended 12 feet past the wall they were sitting on. Building code only allows an engineered truss to overhang by 3 feet before it needs a structural wall or a header to sit on.

Nobody had drawn that in. So I called up the builder and said, let's get a header in here: three ply, 11 7/8 inch. I knew that spanning 20 feet it could carry the heel loads on the truss engineering plan, which is the total weight a truss can bear.

The plans weren't wrong, exactly. They just stopped short of the part that mattered, and somebody had to notice.

## The storefront version

The one that comes up most now is product grouping. A merchant tells us all their variants are on their products. Then you look at the store and they aren't. Each color is its own product, with sizes as its variants. And what they want is for those products to be recommended together, without duplicates.

It's the same problem as the porch. What I was told and what's actually there are two different things, and the build has to work with what's there.

## What carried over

I take in the big picture of a project before I dissect it into smaller parts.

With a house, I'd look at the whole plan first and then work out the order we had to build in: basement walls, then sill plates, then the floor system, then the wall system, then the roof system. Each step sits on the one before it.

I do the same thing in software. Start at the highest level, then work down.

## What didn't

Going head first into it, as fast as possible. That's the habit I had to leave on the jobsite.
